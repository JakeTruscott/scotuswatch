\section{Introduction}

{\Large \textbf{\underline{Key Findings in the Stat Pack for the 2025-26 term}}} \par \vspace{1cm}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \Large \textbf{Roberts and Kavanaugh at the Helm}. \normalsize Chief Justice Roberts and Justice Kavanaugh tied for the top frequencies in the majority, followed by Justice Barrett. Roberts and Kavanaugh were both in the majority 95\% of the time while Barrett was in the majority 92\% of the time. This ordering is the same for frequencies in the majority in non-unanimous and closely divided cases. \par \vspace{2.5mm}

\item \Large \textbf{Ideological Splits in Closely Divided Cases}. \normalsize 1 in 5 of the Court’s decisions this term were decided 6-3 along ideological lines. This far exceeds the 11\% average for the 2020-2021 through 2024-2025 terms. \par \vspace{2.5mm}

\item \Large \textbf{Birds of the Same Feather (Vote Together)}. \normalsize Multiple justice pairs agreed at the highest level. Across all cases, Justices Kagan and Sotomayor, Kavanaugh and (CJ) Roberts, Jackson and Sotomayor, and Thomas and Alito all agreed 94\% of the time. \par \vspace{2.5mm}

\item \Large \textbf{Justices Thomas and Jackson are (Again) Prolific Writers}. \normalsize  Justice Thomas authored the most opinions this term with 28 (total) and the most concurrences with 15, while Justice Jackson authored the second most with 26 (total) but had the most dissents with 10. Chief Justice Roberts authored the fewest with 6 total (all majority opinions). \par \vspace{2.5mm}

\item \Large \textbf{Some (Very) Long Opinions}. \normalsize The longest overall decision this term was \emph{Trump v. Barbara} (Birthright Citizenship) at over 60,000 total words, while the longest individual opinions were Justice Thomas’ dissent in \emph{Barbara} and Justice Kavanaugh’s dissent in \emph{Learning Resources} (Tariffs). Chief Justice Roberts averaged the longest opinions this term at approximately 7,100 words. Moreover, 4 of the 5 longest individual opinions this term were dissents.

\end{enumerate}
